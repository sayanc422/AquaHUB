## Wikimedia Commons batch, 17–18 September 2026

32 of the 45 products missing a photograph got one this way.

**Verification protocol, every image, no exceptions:** queried the Commons API directly
(`action=query&generator=search...&prop=imageinfo&iiprop=url|extmetadata|size`), read
`extmetadata.LicenseShortName` off the response, and accepted only `cc0`, public domain, or an
explicit `cc-by`/`cc-by-sa` variant. Anything containing "nc" (non-commercial) or "nd"
(no-derivatives) was rejected outright, and anything with no license metadata at all was rejected —
a Commons search result is not itself a licence. Artist/attribution came from the same
`extmetadata` response, not guessed from a filename.

**13 products were searched for and NOT given an image**, on purpose, rather than forcing a
mismatched or under-licensed one through: `bristlenose-pleco`, `otocinclus`, `saulosi`,
`acei-yellow-tail`, `yellow-shrimp`, `red-melon-badis` (fish/inverts — either no correctly-identified
Commons photo exists at all, or the only ones that do are below the 1200×900 floor, or the only
adequately-sized ones are mislabeled/unidentified at species level), and `seiryu-stone-5kg`,
`canister-filter-400lph`, `heater-100w`, `master-test-kit`, `community-flake-100g`,
`algae-wafers-250g`, `frozen-bloodworm-100g` (hardscape/equipment/food — Commons skews toward
educational/nature photography and is genuinely sparse for generic retail product shots; several
searches for these returned nothing but unrelated PDFs and scanned books). These stay `NULL` and
render as the designed placeholder rather than a wrong or risky photograph.

**Two identification caveats and one crop repair are recorded inline in the table above**
(`blue-velvet-shrimp`, `ghost-shrimp`, `mystery-snail`'s synonym note, `malaysian-trumpet-snail`'s
crop fix) — read those rows, not just this summary, before using this batch as a precedent for a
future one.

## What "unverified" means here

These were supplied for design work and are in the repository so the site can be built and reviewed
against real photographs instead of grey boxes. None of them has a licence on record.

Before the shop takes a single order, every row above needs to become one of:

* **own** — photographed by the shop. Best answer: it also shows the actual fish for sale, which is
  what a customer is buying.
* **licensed** — a stock licence covering commercial use, with the licence number in this table.
* **breeder** — supplied by the breeder or importer with written permission, filed.

Aquarium photography is routinely watermarked and routinely enforced, and a shop is a commercial use
with no fair-dealing argument available.

## Processing applied

Originals were 5.6 MB across ten files. Each was resized to 1600 px wide at most, re-encoded as
progressive JPEG at quality 82 (76 for `auratus.jpg`, whose rock background compresses badly), and
**stripped of EXIF** — camera metadata can carry GPS coordinates and an owner's name, which a shop
has no reason to publish. Total is now 1.8 MB.

Two are below the 1200 px minimum in `README.md` and should be re-shot:

* `nkhomo-benga-peacock.jpg` — 1136 px wide. Acceptable on a card, soft as a hero.
* `salvini.jpg` — **474 px wide.** Too small for anything but a thumbnail.
